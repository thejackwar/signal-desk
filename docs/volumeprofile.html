Volume profile.

How much traded at each price, not just at each time. The busiest prices show where buyers and sellers agreed, and they often act as support and resistance.

How to read it

A normal volume bar tells you when shares traded. A volume profile turns it sideways and tells you at what price.

1Point of control (POC)

The single price with the most volume: the price the market agreed on most. Price often gets pulled back to it.

2Value area

The range around the POC holding about 70% of the volume, between the value area high (VAH) and low (VAL). Inside it is "fair"; outside it is a price the market is testing.

3High and low volume nodes

Thick parts of the profile (HVNs) are busy prices where price tends to slow down or stall. Thin parts (LVNs) were traded little, and price usually moves through them fast.

4Naked POCs

A past session's point of control that price has not traded at since. Many traders watch them as targets, because unfinished business often gets revisited.

How traders use it

  1. Back to value: when price leaves the value area and fails to hold outside it, traders look for a move back to the POC.
  2. Breakout from value: when price leaves the value area and holds outside it, the old VAH or VAL often becomes support or resistance.
  3. Through the thin zones: a break into a low volume node often travels quickly to the next high volume node.

Each bar's volume is spread evenly across its high–low range. With 1–5 minute bars (the last session and 5 days) this is very close to a profile built from every single trade; longer periods use longer bars, so their levels are a little less exact. A volume profile shows where trading happened, not where price will go. For information only. Not investment advice.
