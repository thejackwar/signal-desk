# News study: what we found (4 Nov 2026)

Full tables: README.md (regenerated by study.py). Data: Benzinga headlines via Alpaca (2019-2026,
scored by FinBERT) and GDELT worldwide news tone and article counts (2019-2026). Signals are used
only from the close after the news; costs included; fixed rules, judged on 2019-23 and 2024-now.

**Same day: yes, news and prices move together.** Strongest for crypto (rank correlation about 0.34
across 11 coins), weak for stocks (0.08), almost none for gold, silver and oil. Mostly the headlines
are describing a move that already happened, so this can't be traded.

**Next 1-10 days: almost nothing.**
- Stocks (S&P 100): no signal predicts. Buying the best-news stocks did the same as buying the
  worst-news ones (5 days: +19.0% vs +18.7% a year), so the small lead over the equal-weight index is
  from favouring heavily covered megacaps, not from the news.
- Gold, silver, oil: nothing, from either Benzinga or world news. Oil's tone even flips sign between
  the two halves.
- Bitcoin: stepping aside for 5 days after a loud bad-news day beat holding in both halves, with
  Benzinga (+42.6% vs +41.6%, +26.2% vs +23.6% a year before costs) and, separately, with GDELT world
  news (+42.7% vs +41.6%, +26.9% vs +23.9% after costs). Two independent sources agreeing makes it
  more believable, but it rests on ~35 days and small margins. Live as "News Step-Aside (Bitcoin)",
  in the Vault (engine/news_signal.py, bot_strategies.btc_news_step_aside).
- S&P 500 (SPY), world news: contrarian. Gloomy market news was followed by better SPY returns
  (IC -0.11 over 5 days, -0.15 over 10, same sign in both halves and stronger since 2024; gloomiest
  fifth of days +0.72% over 5 days vs +0.32% on average). Not yet tested as a strategy.

GDELT note: its API throttles GitHub's runners hard; fetch_gdelt.py asks for the whole span in one
request per theme with long back-off, which took about 4 hours but got through.
